## Architecture and threat model

The actual application uses a custom HTTP router, authenticated `Actor`, service-role Supabase repository adapters, and SQL RPCs against `app_private`. Consequently RLS alone is not authorization: every RPC must validate the active actor, current business membership, action role, and relationship to the exact object. Frontend guards are usability controls, not security boundaries. Marketplace manager/staff/viewer permissions are separate from project editing and platform staff privileges.

Protected assets include immutable RFQ specifications, supplier prices and drafts, private conversations and files, production agreements, proofs, transfer reports, and case-scoped staff access records. Threat actors include an unrelated authenticated workspace, an invited supplier attempting to see a competitor, a removed or downgraded member with cached data or an old token, an insufficiently privileged staff member, and concurrent/retried clients with stale state.

Existing request publication explicitly grants supplier access. Notifications never grant access. A supplier must not become a member of the buyer workspace. Public supplier/RFQ data must not expose original design assets, full addresses, financial details, or private production files. Billing orders are unrelated to production orders; transfer reports do not verify a bank payment.
